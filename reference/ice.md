## 5.

**GEOSCIENCE AUSTRALIA — PRELIMINARY EVENT NOTE**
**Internal. Not for release.**

The 1522Z event is not an earthquake.

Energy sits between 20 and 200 seconds. No impulsive onset — amplitude rises over four minutes and decays across twenty. No coherent P arrival at any station in the network. Long-period surface waves dominate, and the radiation pattern is consistent with a horizontal force sustained over minutes rather than a slip on a fault.

We record it as a submarine sediment failure on the Prydz Channel Fan, initiating at the shelf break and continuing to move for the duration of the signal.

The estimate of runout distance is not a typographical error. **We put the transport at 700 to 900 kilometres.**

A source that translates that far radiates as a source hundreds of kilometres wide. Bulletin 003 reasoned correctly from a narrow source. This is not one.

Recommend immediate reinstatement of warning for the entire northern Indian Ocean rim. Estimated arrival, Maldives, 0145Z. Estimated arrival, Gulf of Kutch, 0450Z.

The time is now 15:44Z.

## 10.

**IOTWMS — BULLETIN, DAY 3**
**Bay of Bengal**

Second slow event, 0712Z, upper Bengal Fan, 18.4°N 89.1°E. Long-period signature, no impulsive onset, consistent with sediment failure and not with rupture.

Analysts asked how a slope 9,000 km from Prydz Bay fails three days after it. The answer appears to be the wave itself.

A gravity wave 200 km long crossing a continental slope swings bottom pressure by the full height of its water column — several metres of head, applied over ten minutes, then withdrawn, then applied again, for six hours. On a fan already at its angle of repose, that is a loading cycle. The Bengal Fan is the largest sediment body on Earth and it has been accumulating since the Himalaya began to rise.

Arrival, Sundarbans and Meghna estuary, 0910Z. Arrival, Chennai, 1040Z.

We are issuing this bulletin thirty-one minutes before the first of those.

## 11.

**SEISMOLOGICAL BULLETIN — DAY 5**

```
Origin      1849:22Z
Location    25.1°N  62.4°E   Makran subduction zone
Depth       21 km
Magnitude   Mw 8.2
```

Thrust mechanism, shallow, marine. Tsunamigenic. This margin last ruptured in 1945 and the interval since is consistent with the recurrence estimate. The segment was late.

Attribution is not available and will not be. A fault at the end of its cycle fails on its own schedule, and the coincidence of five days cannot be resolved into cause. What can be stated is that the stress required to advance the clock on a mature fault is small — ocean tides modulate crustal stress by one to ten kilopascals and demonstrably modulate seismicity — and that global sea level has risen 6 cm in five days.

Six centimetres of water is 0.6 kilopascals, applied everywhere, and not withdrawn.

We note further that the Makran coast was struck by the Prydz wave on Day 1 and that the population had not returned.

## 12.

**US GEOLOGICAL SURVEY / NOAA — JOINT ADVISORY, DAY 8**

Slope failure, 0341Z, Currituck sector, 36.4°N 74.7°W, continental slope off the Virginia–North Carolina margin. Volume order 100 km³. This slope has failed before and the deposit has been mapped since the 1980s.

Advisory in effect: Virginia Beach, the Outer Banks, Hampton Roads, Delaware Bay approaches. Estimated arrival 0510Z to 0620Z. Amplitude estimates 2 to 6 m, higher in Chesapeake approaches.

Until this morning, the North Atlantic had recorded no damage from any part of this sequence and coverage in this hemisphere had treated it as an Indian Ocean event.

We direct attention to a finding that has not been communicated adequately. Sea level rise sourced from Antarctica is **not uniform**. The ice sheet's own mass currently attracts the ocean toward it; removing that mass lowers sea level near the source and raises it in the far field by 10 to 30 per cent above the global mean. The Northern Hemisphere receives the largest rise of any latitude band on Earth.

The hemisphere that was not struck is the hemisphere being loaded hardest.

## 14.

**Handwritten, on the reverse of the Day 10 IERS bulletin. Author not identified.**

I keep the sheet on the desk and I keep coming back to the last line. *These quantities have no consequence.* Which is true. Nobody drowned because the day got longer.

But the axis is not where it was. It is eighty centimetres off, and it is going, and the reason it is going is that the weight has come off one end of the planet and gone into the water everywhere else — and *that* is the quantity with consequence, and the pole is simply the only instrument honest enough to show it as a single number.

We have all been reading this fortnight as a sequence of events. Norway on the third. Virginia on the eighth. Makran, the Fan, the Maldives. Fourteen entries, and the shape of a list is the shape of something that ends, so everyone has been asking which margin is next, as though the answer were a place.

It is not a list. It is a load, and the load is still arriving. Fifteen centimetres so far, and the grounding line is retreating into a basin that deepens inland, which means it does not stop where it is and there is no configuration it can find until the bed comes back up, and the bed does not come back up for six hundred kilometres. Three metres, eventually. Thirty kilopascals on every square metre of every ocean floor on Earth. Every fan, every flank, every mature fault, pressed a little harder and never let go.

Not fourteen days. Fourteen days is the part we were awake for.

My granddaughter is four. She will spend her whole life inside this, and it will not once present itself to her as news.
