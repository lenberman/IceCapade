## S1 · Waiākea · `2031-05-14T17:20:00-10:00`

Nālani, first person, past tense — **opening two paragraphs in present tense, deliberate.**

Tūtū in her chair watching the clouds as the sun descends. Lehua reading at the table. Nālani frying Spam musubi in a glaze for Lehua's dessert, burning her hand, calling her *the silent one* to her great-grandmother and asking her for ice. Lehua refuses, complies, sets the table. **The table is set for three:** Ikaika at school, Tomás six years gone.

Then the catechism. Nālani quizzes Lehua and Lehua has all of it — the bay empties, the water returns angry, it sweeps everything below a certain point. **Waiākea** by name. 1946, **Tūtū at six**, her father walking down to look at the reef, the bottom of the bay, fish standing on their sides, no one knowing, many dead. Tūtū supplies 1960: they knew by then, sixty died anyway, the store went, the streets are gone forever. *"We walked those streets together." / "You walked. I sat."*

Lehua: **"Mom. Is that why you do the library thing?"** — never answered.

**Bzzzz**, bold italic, standalone, as a section break.

**Oscar** — her LLM assistant, set up years ago when her grant proposals stopped being funded, to watch hydroacoustic feeds for ice. *Many watched them, few watched for ice.* Four alerts in six years: a blue whale on a bad bearing, a seamount venting, twice clock drift. She sends Lehua to clean up, meets Tūtū's steady gaze, takes the phone to her room and closes the door.

**The investigation is typed**, monospaced on the page. `Explain alert` — HA01 Cape Leeuwin, T04:12:07Z, descending sweep 11 Hz to 4 Hz over 340 s. `Amplitude` / `Again` — three orders of magnitude too large. `Current HA01 waveform` — railed; HA01 is a dead hydrophone, and **she is right**. `Examine Crozet hydrophones, HA04` — Crozet has it, appropriately late, not railed, *just unimaginable.* `Display possible source locii` → a hyperboloid; `Display surface intersection` → a hyperbola through Prydz Bay, event at twenty past three, fifty-two minutes before Leeuwin. `What is coming out of Prydz Bay?` — nothing since 03:20; Davis stopped at 03:19; Zhongshan, Progress and Bharati at 03:20. *Antarctic telemetry drops. It drops for weather and it drops for satellite windows.* She does not register the synchrony.

*"I closed Oscar, the screen and laptop. I needed to share."* Then: Tūtū at the window, and *"She looked at me a while longer and turned back to the window, where there was nothing new to see."*

## S2 · Silver Spring · `2031-05-15T07:00:00-04:00`

Daniel, close third, past tense, with free-indirect intrusions (*Imagine, he thought.*).

**The Grier marriage is not good.** This overrides everything the old spine said about it, and it is now the fact.

- **The family has abandoned the table.** They eat at the counter. They moved there when **Eve stopped sitting with them** — her senior year, preferring proximity to the toaster to her parents. *He was saddened but he understood.*
- **Daniel has "his end of the counter."** *His end of the counter. Imagine, he thought.* Ellen keeps to her end, beyond Eve's spot. They conduct the scene at opposite ends of a worktop.
- He greets her — *"Hello, my dear. Did you sleep well?"* — and she does not answer it. *"I was up all night thinking about what needs to be done."*
- **Ellen makes plenty.** The money is hers. The cottage is hers — **five generations**.
- **Eve is a high-school senior**, so seventeen or eighteen. She enters with *"Good morning Mater and Pater,"* takes a bagel from the toaster and retreats to **the empty table**, face down in a tablet. **Ellen ignores her and proceeds.**
- Numbers: deferred maintenance **almost a hundred K**; Sound Atlantic dropping them **September 1**, *a portfolio-level reassessment of coastal exposure*, not claims; the federal program takes them at nearly four times unless they elevate, and elevating is **almost another hundred**.
- Eve asks **"How much is it worth?"** Daniel answers her question with *"It's been in your family for 5 generations"* and then **takes her side**: *"I think Eve's is a reasonable question."* Ellen refuses it: *"Well, no matter what, we have to do the deferred maintenance."*
- Ellen's contact at **American Life** wants a high-level climate analyst. *"Shall I send you a link to the posting?"* — **"Sure."**

### Two things the manuscript now does that the spine did not

**The tables rhyme.** S1 ends with three people eating at a table set for three. S2 opens on a table nobody sits at, in a house that moved to the counter because the daughter stopped coming. Two households, one page apart, and the difference between them is stated entirely in furniture. Nothing should be added to point at it.

**"Sure" is the whole character.** He says it to a job he will not take. In the office beat he concurs with reasoning he has not tested. Same syllable, same man, ninety minutes apart — and the second one costs a coastline. This rhyme exists *because* the marriage went cold; the good-marriage version could not reach it.

# Cast

**Dr. Nālani Akana**, 44. Harvard undergrad, Scripps PhD on cryogenic signals in IMS hydroacoustic data — iceberg tremor at HA01 Cape Leeuwin. Married at Scripps to **Tomás Reyes**; two children born while she was a graduate student. At completion she turned down SOEST at Mānoa and a NOAA slot at PMEL and came home to Hilo — **a decision made married, at 29, with her husband agreeing and giving up his own better options.** She moved in with Tūtū Ruth; four people in one house ever since.

What Hilo gets her: a 3-3 load, two hydrophones on borrowed ship time, the annual tsunami-awareness talk at the library, and **Oscar**. What it costs her is the whole first act.

The reason she came home is on the page in S1 and it is Tūtū's tales — inherited memory, not a career calculation and not Tomás. The *order* still governs: the decision was joint, at 29, Tomás alive.

Tomás died six years before Day 0 — ordinary, slow, eighteen months. She has not remarried and the book does not stage this as a question. His model code is still on her machine, commented in his hand, and she still runs it. **The book does not explain this or apologize for it.**

**Tūtū Ruth**, 91. **Six at the 1946 wave**, born 1939–40. The family store on Kamehameha Avenue went in 1960 and was never rebuilt; it is parkland now and Nālani can stand on the grass where the register sat. *Tūtū* is Hawaiian for grandmother, not a name — inside the house she is **Tūtū**, narration and outsiders say Tūtū Ruth, and unrelated locals may call her Tūtū too.

**Ikaika Reyes**, 19. Away at university on the mainland at Day 0 — a voice on the phone during the worst night, unable to get home, which rhymes against the choice his mother made at his age and will not be pointed at.

**Lehua Reyes**, 16. At Hilo High, in the house. Her silence in S1 is **directed**: she withholds domestic compliance from her mother and hands her the whole catechism in the same scene. Not withdrawn — sixteen and selective. Any later scene that reads her as generally uncommunicative gets it wrong.

**Daniel Grier**, 51. Deputy director, NOAA Tsunami Program, Silver Spring. Oceanography PhD, then four years as a Scripps postdoc overlapping Nālani's doctorate in the same building; he knew Tomás as well as he knew her. **His own science never landed** — two papers that went nowhere, a grant declined twice, a second postdoc as the only offer — and he took the government instead and has never once called that a defeat out loud.

Six years as a PTWC duty scientist in his thirties: the 24/7 rotation, the seismic screen, the fifteen minutes between an earthquake and a warning. Eve was born during that posting. Then Silver Spring and eleven years of climbing carefully. Nine years ago he sat on the panel that scored Nālani's array proposal out of funding — *insufficient institutional leverage*, which meant she wouldn't move to Mānoa. He remembers writing it, and she has had his cell number the whole time.

Competent, careful, institutionally fluent, and aware that fluency is what he traded ambition for. **He agrees with things.** That is the character, and S2 establishes it in a kitchen before the book charges him for it. Whether he went to Tomás's funeral is open — Dial 8.

**Ellen Grier**, 49. Health-policy lobbyist, K Street. Married Daniel at the end of the Scripps years, when he still looked like a scientist with a future. **She makes plenty.** The cottage on the Currituck Banks is hers, five generations of it.

**The marriage is cold** — see ESTABLISHED. Not hostile, not cruel, and there is no affair and no crisis. They are courteous, they are at opposite ends of the counter, and she does not answer when he asks how she slept. She still finds him jobs. He still says *sure*. Her grievance was always his lack of ambition; what the manuscript adds is that the grievance has finished arguing and become the weather.

**Eve Grier**, high-school senior, 17–18. Their daughter, born on Oʻahu, no memory of it. Watchful, keeps score, arch — *Mater and Pater*. She stopped sitting with her parents, which is why the family eats standing up, and she is the only one who asks what the house is worth. POV from S20 onward.

**Keala Fonoti**, 20. Undergrad at the S7 whiteboard; Hilo-raised, attended Nālani's library talks as a schoolchild. Becomes Nālani's graduate student, then her successor in the lab.

**Marcus Yee**, 55. Curtin University, Perth — the HA01 side of the ocean. Nālani's longest-running colleague and, in Part Four, her designated skeptic: he doubts the Lānaʻi deposits and doubts the creep correlation, correctly by the literature, in the two disputes `Hawaii.md` §5 says must not resolve.

**E. Sandoval**, AFTAC duty analyst, Patrick SFB. Screens the Day 0 hydroacoustic signals as non-nuclear — correctly, immediately — and files them. Two documents, no scenes. The second is unbearable because the first was right.

**Oscar.** Nālani's LLM assistant. **"It," never "he."** She named it and has no illusions about it; the book has exactly one machine with a masculine pronoun and that is Tomás's model code. **Typed, never spoken.** Instrument, never oracle: it fetches, computes and reports, and never interprets. It must never notice anything before a human does, and it does not run Tomás's model.

**Edgar.** Daniel's assistant — institutional where Oscar is personal. Set on the page in the **Analyst** style: sans, small, indented, ruled above and below, against Oscar's typewriter. Both men type to their machines in the same monospaced bold; the difference the book states is not how they are addressed but what talks back. **Daniel and Edgar work in UTC** — see Ledger #37.

**Supporting:** **Kawika Pang**, Hilo civil defense, who trusts Nālani because she has shown up for eleven straight years; Daniel's boss, an SES careerist who owns the word "premature."

## The two households

The book's real symmetry, and S1–S2 already state it.

Nālani's house has four people in it and a table set for three, and the sixteen-year-old who will not fetch ice can recite what the ocean does to Waiākea. Memory is transmitted; the household is crowded; the daughter withholds compliance and gives over the inheritance.

Daniel's house has three people in it and a table nobody uses. The daughter left it first and the parents followed her to the counter. Nothing is transmitted. The seventeen-year-old asks the only unsentimental question in the room and her mother talks past her.

One household will lose a harbour and keep everything else. The other will lose a house and discover it had already lost the rest.

## Daniel and Nālani

The old spine argued that a good marriage made the pull between them harder and better. That argument is void.

What survives, and is stronger: Nālani finds Daniel's carefulness to be the whole of his integrity, and she is right about that in the only fortnight when the careful reading is also the correct one. Ellen finished finding it insufficient some years ago. Both women are describing the same trait.

Nālani's marriage ended by death, not by choice or failure. She is not looking and the book should not stage her as available. What she is is *understood*, professionally, for the first time in fifteen years, by a man who was in the room when she was still going to be somebody.

**Dial 1 needs rethinking** — see Dials.

## Narrative mode

Nālani: **first person, past tense**, with S1's present-tense opening as a deliberate exception. Daniel: **close third, past**, with free-indirect intrusions. Eve's, when Part Four opens her up, close third unless the drafting says otherwise.

The asymmetry does real work: she is the book's voice and he is its case study, which is the right relation between the woman who understood it first and the man who signed the cancellation. It also puts the reader inside the only person who is right, so the first act's helplessness is felt rather than observed.

Alongside both, the *Fourteen Days* found documents carry the planet while the scenes carry the people. The reader sometimes meets the document first and the scene behind it a chapter later — Bulletin 003's cancellation, then Daniel drafting the concurrence.

Part Four: time-jump chapters, years apart, documents continuing. Each chapter is one scene on one day; the jumps do the summarizing.

Open: what voice the S26 coda takes, since it belongs to Lehua.

# PART ONE — THE DAY

**S1 · Waiākea — WRITTEN.** See ESTABLISHED.

**S2 · Silver Spring — IN PROGRESS.** Breakfast written; the office beat remains. Four notices from four agencies, the desk consensus assembling — floating ice, Larsen B precedent, displaces its own weight, no seismic source, no wave — every sentence true, and he concurs. Draft in `S2-suggestions.md`.
*Function: put Daniel on the record agreeing with the reasoning that becomes Bulletin 003. His error is not negligence. That is the point.*

### S3 · D0, mid-morning EDT · phone · both
**Not a cold call.** She has had his cell number for twenty years — an old friend rung by a woman who knew him when he was still going to be a scientist, who does not have to say *you reviewed my array proposal*, so does not, and it sits inside the call the whole way through.

She makes the four-pins case: four sectors, one day, a circumpolar sequence — and then the thing nobody has a desk for. Trough-mouth fans, loaded to the angle of repose, pore pressure re-equilibrating on a clock of hours. **"I'm not calling about the ice. I'm calling about the mud."** He gives her the textbook, correctly. Somewhere in it he asks after the kids and means it, and the question costs the call ninety seconds it did not have.

After he hangs up he writes *pore pressure?* on a legal pad and does nothing with it, and the doing-nothing is a scene, not a summary.

### S4 · D0, afternoon · Patrick SFB, then Silver Spring · document + Daniel
Sandoval's AFTAC screening note as a document: non-nuclear, correctly discriminated, no further action — the treaty machine working exactly as designed and therefore telling no one. Then Daniel in the ops room as the Indian Ocean models converge on sub-decimetre amplitudes and the cancellation goes out. He drafts the US concurrence himself. The chapter ends on his signature.

### S5 · D0, evening (rolling) · document interlude — the pulse
A weather-hobbyist forum thread, raw. Japan logs a clean 1.5 hPa blip; then Perth, Réunion, Nairobi, the Azores — timestamps marching westward at 315 m/s, hobbyists cross-posting screenshots, someone starting a shared spreadsheet, nobody knowing what it is a dataset *of*. One poster notes that Bharati would normally catch this and Bharati is not posting. Thread locked for speculation. Last visible post: *whatever it was, it was everywhere.*

# PART TWO — THE MUD

### S6 · D0, night EDT · Silver Spring ops floor · Daniel
The Prydz fan lets go eleven hours after the ice. Daniel is in the building on an unrelated pretext when the Arabian Sea gauges start posting. Jamnagar. Karachi. The room goes quiet, which is worse than erupting. Someone prints his concurrence memo unasked and he understands it has become an exhibit.

He calls the only person who was right, and it is harder than a cold call would have been, because he has to be wrong in front of somebody who watched him choose this life. She answers on the first ring and does not say I told you so, because she is already on the next problem.

### S7 · D0, ~8 pm HST · UH Hilo lab · Nālani
The whiteboard. Nālani, Keala and one other undergrad run the circumpolar arithmetic and the pore-pressure clocks. **The model is Tomás's** — his code, his comments, his variable names, a header block with his initials and a date from before the children could read. Nobody remarks on it. Keala notices the initials and does not ask.

She writes the failure windows for the Crary, Ross and Belgica fans on **one sheet of paper** — 6.6 / 7.5 / 4.6 h — photographs it, and sends it to Daniel's personal phone before the second fan lets go. Observed: 6.75 / 6.4 / 5.0. The sheet is time-stamped by the send. Nobody says the word credential.

### S8 · D0, 9 pm HST → D1 pre-dawn · Hilo · Nālani
The evacuation, a household before it is a coastline. Kawika Pang takes her call at 9 pm because it is her; the routes she has walked with library audiences for eleven years hold; PTWC gets its warning from a woman its parent agency declined to fund.

Then her own three. Ikaika on the phone from the mainland, four hours behind understanding, on speaker on the kitchen counter while they pack. Lehua carrying her great-grandmother's bag and being sixteen about it. **Tūtū directs the packing** — photographs, the koa bowl, nothing else — and Nālani, running a county's evacuation from a cell phone, does what her grandmother says without arguing. *"We've done this."* Lehua has not. Ikaika hears it happen through a phone.

### S9 · D1, ~02:40 HST · Hilo · Nālani
The harbor event, per `Hawaii.md`: the bay finds its note and rings — not one wave but a seiche, building for hours, the harbor destroying itself in the dark by increments. The water crosses the breakwater, takes the port, floods the bayfront parkland, and stops roughly at the grass line where the town already retreated.

Dawn: Nālani at the edge of the flooded park on the spot where the store stood, ocean on one side, living town on the other. Lehua with her; Tūtū in the car because she has seen it twice. Casualties in single digits. The harbor is gone; Hilo is not.

### S10 · D1 · document interlude — Lisbon
One page. A civil-protection communiqué or a wire report from the Praça do Comércio — the square rebuilt after 1755 taking water again, 276 years later, from a cause no one in 1755 could have conceived. A single line invoking Voltaire, or pointedly refusing to.

### S11 · D1, ~13:00 EDT · Washington / Annapolis · Daniel
The Crary wave per `EastCoast.md`: two to four metres at the open coast, the Sandy comparison, **Norfolk flooded**, the Chesapeake's bent shallow water stripping the wave before it reaches DC. His own house is dry and he finds the dryness obscurely shameful. Eve is at a friend's in Annapolis — real fear, brief peril, resolved in an hour; use it lightly.

**Ellen's first question, before Eve is confirmed safe: "What about Corolla?"** The cottage takes water and stands. That is the trap: it stands.

# PART THREE — THE WEEK

### S12 · D+2–D+5 · Silver Spring / Hilo, by phone · both
Opens with the Bengal Fan — the passing wave as a loading event, the mechanism named aloud — then Norway on D+3, and the pattern stops being deniable. Daniel converts disgrace into mandate: a fan-watch task force, Nālani as its scientific anchor, by phone, unpaid at first because the paperwork moves slower than the mud. The 3 am calls acquire a rhythm. Behind one of them the returning Lamb pulse crosses both barometers, noted in a sentence, planted at S5.

They talk pore pressure and then keep talking. **What Ellen notices, and what it now means, needs rewriting against the cold marriage.** The old version had her un-jealous because the marriage was good. The available version is bleaker: she notices that at 3 am her husband sounds like a man who wants something, and has not heard him sound that way in her direction in years, and does not raise it.

### S13 · D+4 · document — Sandoval, second appearance
Sandoval forwards the complete Day 0 SEL sequence to the new task force, unsolicited, one-line cover: all four events, correctly discriminated, correctly filed. Handwritten postscript — *"You'll want the rest of these. They were never wrong. Neither were we."*

### S14 · D+5–D+7 · Silver Spring · Daniel
Makran nearly kills the Currituck decision: Daniel's boss reads it as proof that background seismicity is noise the task force is pattern-matching into signal. The attribution argument — 1–10 kPa of tidal modulation against 0.6 kPa of new load — happens in front of him and settles nothing, which is the point.

Then Currituck. Nālani's bottom-pressure argument; no agency owning the decision; **Daniel forcing it** — the same gate that failed open on Day 0, now his to hold shut, in public, against his boss's *premature*, with his name on it either way. A partial evacuation order goes out for the Outer Banks and lower Chesapeake.

Ellen drives to Corolla anyway, to empty the cottage. She has spent a week watching him be careful in public and does not experience her own husband as an authority; she thinks there is time; he would have said there was time too, if she had asked. She did not ask. Eve goes with her. He finds out from Eve's text.

### S15 · D+8 · Currituck Banks / Silver Spring · both households
**Friday 23 May 2031. USNA commissioning that morning.** The margin fails around 14:00 EDT; Corolla takes it at ~15:04; **Naval Station Norfolk at ~16:16**, for the second time in eight days. Annapolis is 232 km up a shallow bay and sees something modest after dark — so the Academy is not inundated, and the image is the families of the class commissioned that morning watching the largest naval base in the world take water on their phones.

Ellen and Eve are on the causeway inland with five generations of a family in the back seat, **because his evacuation order emptied the roads a day early**: the order he could not make her believe is the reason she lives. The cottage goes entirely — not flooded, gone, the lot resurveyed by the ocean.

That night, all three Griers in the Silver Spring kitchen. *Staging: end on an action, not a verdict. Who leaves the room first is decided later.* **With the marriage already cold, this scene is no longer where it breaks — it is where they find out there was less to break than they thought.**

### S16 · D+10 · document — the number
IERS Rapid Service bulletin: length of day +0.35 ms; polar motion displaced. Reproduced in full. Beneath it, a paragraph in Nālani's voice — an email to Keala's cohort — saying this is the only single number that expresses the whole thing, that it has no consequence whatsoever, and that both halves of that sentence are true.

### S17 · D+12 · split scene, phone · both — PART THREE CODA
The hinge. **Tuesday 27 May, the day after Memorial Day.** Gauges are back in service and none return to predicted level. Nālani on the lava shore at night, Daniel in the dark ops room, the same residual on both screens. One of them says: **"It's not going back down."**

## Dials

**Staging in LyX.** Undecided dials that come down to alternative *text* live as LyX Branches declared in the master, named for the **state**: `AffairConsummated`, `MarriageBreaks`, `DanielAtFuneral`, `HilinaFires`. Use an inverted inset for the other version so one checkbox flips between two drafts. Dial 6 spans chapters and is better handled as alternative child files. A branch tests which version reads better; it is not a way to ship both.

**1. The affair — reopened.** The old recommendation (unconsummated, because there is no marital vacuum to explain it) rested on the good marriage, which is gone. There is now a vacuum. That does not make consummation right — it makes it *available*, which is a different and worse reason to reach for it. The stronger version may now be the opposite of before: with a cold marriage at home, a man who says *sure* to everything declining the one thing he actually wants is the most characteristic act available to him. Undecided.

**2. The marriage — reframed again.** It cannot break by attrition because attrition has already happened. What is left is whether either of them ever says so. S15's kitchen is no longer the break point; S20 and S21 remain candidates, and so does never.

**3. Ending point.** Decided: stabilization (S26). The novella exit (S9) and hinge exit (S17) remain marked.

**4. Sandoval.** Decided: two documents, no scenes.

**5. Names / cultural read.** Nālani's Native Hawaiian identity is worth keeping for the store-in-the-parkland inheritance and worth a cultural read at manuscript stage, covering Lehua, Ikaika, Keala, Kawika and the *tūtū* usage. Reyes as the children's surname is still a placeholder.

**6. The gun.** (a) **Hilina fires ~Y20, beams Polynesia, mainland gun stays cocked** — recommended. (b) Nuʻuanu or Molokaʻi fires and the Pacific Northwest is struck; bigger, and it buries the retreat material. (c) Nothing fires; the watching is the payoff.

**7. Coda POV.** Recommended: **Lehua**. She compresses the family line and the town's, and was on the page at sixteen for both Hilo nights.

**8. Tomás's funeral.** Did Daniel go? Recommend **no** — he sent an email, he was between postings, it was a long way — and let her not mention it until Part Four.
