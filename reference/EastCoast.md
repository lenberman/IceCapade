# East Coast

Reference note. The Atlantic seaboard and the Crary beam.

**The organising point:** four separate things reach the East Coast, on four different clocks, and only one of them is a wave. The wave is the least important of the four.

| | Arrives | Severity |
|---|---|---|

# 1. The Crary wave — real, but not the end of anything

**Distance:** ~14,000 km from the Crary Fan to the US mid-Atlantic. **Arrival:** roughly 20 hours after the fan fails.

The wave crosses the equator and spreads across the widest part of the Atlantic. By New York it is substantially attenuated.

**The compensating factor is the shelf.** The US eastern seaboard sits behind one of the widest shallow continental shelves in the world. A shelf amplifies through shoaling — but it also dissipates through friction and filters through refraction. The three effects partly cancel.

**Estimate: 2–4 m at the open coast**, more in funnels and resonant harbours.

That floods Lower Manhattan, the tunnels, JFK and LaGuardia, the Jersey shore, the Delaware and Chesapeake approaches. **The honest comparison is Sandy, not an apocalypse.**

**Washington is largely protected.** The Potomac and Chesapeake are long, shallow and bent, which strips energy from a long wave. **Annapolis and Norfolk are fully exposed** — and Norfolk is the largest naval base on Earth, on ground that is also subsiding (see §3).

> **The most useful property of this wave is that it does not settle the argument.**
>
> The East Coast takes a serious hit, survives it, cleans up, and spends the next decade debating whether it was a warning or an outlier. A wave that killed a hundred thousand Americans would end that argument on day one. This one does not, and the argument is the story.

# 2. Currituck — D+8

Already in Timeline.1 (D-12) and far more dangerous than the Crary wave, because it is **local**.

~100 km³ of continental slope off the Virginia–North Carolina margin. Mapped since the 1980s; it has failed before. Warning time is hours, not a day.

**Virginia Beach, the Outer Banks, Hampton Roads.**

This is the event that ends the "someone else's disaster" framing in the American press. Everything before it happened to other people.

# 3. The fingerprint — **build the Eastern arc on this**

Sea level rise from Antarctica is **not uniform**, and the pattern is diagnostic rather than incidental.

An ice sheet is a mass, and mass attracts. Antarctica's ice currently pulls the surrounding ocean toward itself, so the sea stands *higher* near Antarctica than it otherwise would. Remove the ice and that attraction goes with it. Water flows away.

**Sea level near Antarctica falls. The far field receives the global mean plus 10–30%. The maximum sits in the North Atlantic.**

> **New York, barely touched by any wave, receives more permanent sea level rise than Buenos Aires, which was destroyed by one.**

## Two local aggravations stack on top

**Subsidence.** The mid-Atlantic coast is sinking — it sits on the collapsing forebulge left by the last ice age, the bulge that was pushed up around the edge of the Laurentide ice sheet and has been settling ever since. Norfolk and the Chesapeake get land going down while sea goes up. This is why Norfolk already has the highest relative sea level rise rate on the US East Coast, today, before anything in your book happens.

**AMOC slowdown.** A weakening Atlantic overturning circulation is argued to raise sea level along the US Northeast specifically, by redistributing water that the current currently holds offshore. Freshwater capping the Southern Ocean (SY-01 in Decades.md) is exactly the kind of forcing invoked in that argument.

**Flag this one as contested.** The Southern Ocean → AMOC → Northeast US sea level chain is a real line of research and genuinely disputed at every link. Have a character say the connection is plausible and unproven rather than having the narration assert it.

## Stack them

The US Northeast is plausibly at or near the **global maximum** of relative sea level rise. Four metres of global rise could mean five or more at the Battery.

**The hemisphere that watched the waves on television absorbs the largest permanent rise.** This has been in the literature since the 1970s and it is the fact that reframes the politics of the book.

# 4. What actually ends these cities

## Washington — never reached by a wave, drowned anyway

The city sits up a long shallow estuary. No ocean wave will ever get there. What gets there is groundwater and storm surge, and both are already arriving: **the Tidal Basin floods on ordinary high tides today.**

**Its importance is political, not physical.** This is the capital, legislating about coastlines it cannot save, from a city being slowly and unspectacularly waterlogged. Every third-act hearing scene happens in a building whose basement is wet.

## New York — a specific and instructive list

| Asset | Failure mode |
|---|---|
| **The subway** | The whole story. Sandy put water in seven tunnels; salt corrosion took years to repair. Tunnels flood at a defined entrance elevation — binary, not gradual. |
| **JFK and LaGuardia** | Both on fill, both at low elevation |
| **Con Ed coastal substations** | One flood ends them, and they feed everything else |
| **The Battery, Red Hook, Rockaways, Jamaica Bay** | Progressive inundation |

New York has spent roughly $100bn on hardening since 2012. It is better defended than almost anywhere on Earth, and it still loses on this timescale — which is the point worth making. Competence and money are not the binding constraint.

## And the market outruns all of it

**TH-01 from Decades.md, and the single most important entry for the East Coast.**

Insurers withdraw from coastal lines. Without insurance, mortgages become unavailable. Without mortgages, property becomes unsaleable. Values collapse, the tax base collapses, and the municipality can no longer fund the defences that would have kept the water out.

**None of this requires the water to arrive.** It requires only a credible forecast that it will.

The financial threshold fires **decades** before the physical one, and it fires faster, because a belief cascade moves quicker than water. This is already visible in Florida and coastal California at rates of rise an order of magnitude below yours.

**A place can become uninhabitable while remaining dry.**

# 5. Atlantic volcanism

Short answer: **weaker than the Pacific case, but two of the three mechanisms hold, and the third one — flank collapse — is the one that matters for the East Coast.**

## Mechanism 1 — decompression melting: weak here

The Iceland precedent (eruption rate up ~30× after deglaciation) involved **kilometres of ice removed from directly above the volcanoes**. That is megapascals of unloading, applied locally.

Your sea level rise is ~40 kPa — roughly a thousandth of that — and it acts on ocean *floor*, which is the wrong sign for a volcano sitting on it. Loading the seafloor suppresses melt if anything.

**Do not claim increased Atlantic eruption rates from unloading.** The physics runs the wrong way.

**The one real exception is Iceland itself.** Iceland has actual ice caps sitting on actual volcanoes — Vatnajökull over Grímsvötn and Bárðarbunga, Mýrdalsjökull over Katla. Those are thinning under ordinary climate change, independent of your event, and the Iceland mechanism applies to them directly and at full strength. Iceland gets more volcanically active for reasons that have nothing to do with Antarctica, in the same decades, which is a nice coincidence to have available.

## Mechanism 2 — pore pressure in volcanic edifices: **holds**

This is the Hawai'i §3b mechanism, and it transfers cleanly to the Atlantic.

Ocean island volcanoes are permeable, saturated, and internally weakened by hydrothermal alteration left over from when they were hot. Raising sea level raises the water table inside the edifice, which reduces the effective stress holding the rock together.

**Atlantic candidates, all with documented past flank collapses:**

- **Cumbre Vieja, La Palma** (Canaries) — the famous one. A 2001 modelling paper proposed a westward collapse generating a trans-Atlantic megatsunami reaching the US East Coast. **Heavily disputed** — see §6.
- **El Hierro, Tenerife** (Canaries) — El Hierro's El Golfo embayment is a collapse scar; Tenerife has several
- **Fogo, Cape Verde** — the Monte Amarelo collapse is dated and well characterised, with associated deposits on Santiago
- **Pico and Faial, Azores**

**These are the sources that reach the US East Coast directly**, which the Antarctic beam does only weakly. Geometrically they are the Atlantic's Hawai'i.

## Mechanism 3 — increased submarine volcanism from load flexure: marginal

Adding 40 kPa across an ocean basin does flex the lithosphere fractionally. There is literature on sea level and glacial cycles modulating mid-ocean ridge volcanism over tens of thousands of years. On your timescale the effect is negligible. **Skip it.**

## Verdict

Atlantic volcanism does not increase. **Atlantic ocean-island flanks get wetter and weaker**, and one of them failing is the mechanism that puts a real wave on the US East Coast.

# 6. Cumbre Vieja — handle carefully

**This is the most contested claim in tsunami science and you should know both sides before using it.**

**The original claim (Ward & Day, 2001):** a future eruption could detach ~500 km³ of Cumbre Vieja's western flank, generating a wave hundreds of metres high locally and arriving at the US East Coast at tens of metres.

**The objections, which are substantial:**
- Real volcanic flanks tend to fail **piecemeal**, in multiple smaller blocks over hours to days, not as one coherent slab. Piecemeal failure generates far smaller waves.
- The modelled wave is short-wavelength and therefore **dispersive** — the same physics that kills your ice-collapse wave in Sequence.md. It largely disperses before crossing the Atlantic.
- Later modelling gets metre-scale rather than tens-of-metres arrivals in North America.
- The 2021 eruption produced no measurable flank movement, which was widely reported as a reality check.

**How to use it:** this is a gift precisely *because* it is contested. Your book already runs on the theme that everyone was correct and everyone was wrong. A character who dismisses Cumbre Vieja is citing the mainstream position, correctly, right up until the flank goes — and even then the argument continues about whether it went the way Ward and Day said or in the piecemeal way the sceptics predicted, and whether the resulting wave vindicated anyone.

**And your premise gives you something the 2001 paper did not have:** a mechanism for *why now*. Ward and Day had no trigger. You have four metres of sea level pressurising a saturated, hydrothermally altered edifice over decades. That is a better answer to "why would it fail" than anything in the original literature.

# 7. The shape of the Eastern arc

1. **T+20 h** — a serious wave that does not settle anything
2. **D+8** — Currituck, local and nasty, ends the spectator framing
3. **Y1–5** — the discovery that the fingerprint gives the North Atlantic *more* permanent rise than the hemisphere that drowned
4. **Y5–20** — subsidence and possible AMOC contribution stack on top; the Northeast approaches the global maximum
5. **Y10–40** — insurance withdrawal, then mortgages, then the tax base. Cities become unviable while still dry.
6. **Anywhere in Y5–40** — an Atlantic ocean-island flank fails, wetter and weaker than it was, and puts the only large wave of the whole arc onto a coast that has spent decades arguing about the slow one.
