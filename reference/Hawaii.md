# Hawai'i

Reference note. Hawai'i appears three times in this book, in three different roles, and they should not be conflated:

1. **A target** of the Ross beam, at ~T+33 h
2. **The best tsunami resonator on Earth**, which is why the target matters far more than its geometry suggests
3. **A source** — and the source that finally reaches North America

---
---

# 1. Why the Galápagos reasoning does not transfer

The instinct is sound: both are steep volcanic islands rising from the abyss, with almost no continental shelf, so almost no shoaling amplification. On that basis Hawai'i should behave like the Galápagos — 3–4 m, flooded waterfront, wrecked moorings, and a bad week.

**The history says otherwise, and the difference is one bay.**

| Event | Hawai'i outcome |
|---|---|
| 1946 Aleutian | **159 dead in Hawai'i**, most in Hilo. Downtown Hilo destroyed. |
| 1960 Chile | **61 dead in Hilo**, waves ~10 m — while the same wave did little elsewhere in the islands |
| 1975 Hilina (local) | M7.2, ~15 m runup locally, 2 dead, minor damage in California |
| 2011 Tohoku | Damage in the tens of millions; Kona coast hit hardest |

The 1960 line is the important one. **The same wave, the same island chain, radically different outcomes shore to shore.** That is not shoaling. That is resonance.

# 2. Hilo Bay — the drum's favourite note

**Seiching**, from Sequence.md: a bay has a natural period of oscillation, and a tsunami whose period matches it sets up a standing wave that keeps building and sloshing for hours after the wave itself has passed.

Hilo Bay's natural period falls squarely in the tsunami band. It is the textbook global example, cited in every treatment of the phenomenon. The bay is also funnel-shaped and shallowing, which concentrates what the resonance amplifies.

**Consequence for the book:** Hilo can take three to five times what the rest of the archipelago takes from the same wave. A wave that gives Honolulu a bad afternoon gives Hilo a catastrophe. Your characters can model this in advance — the resonance is well characterised and predictable, unlike almost everything else in this story.

**Arrival:** Ross fan → Hilo is ~10,600 km. At √(gh) over ~4,500 m average depth, roughly 15 hours after the fan fails. Overall **~T+33 h**.

## And Hilo already retreated

**Use this.** After 1946 and 1960, the destroyed waterfront downtown was never rebuilt. It is parkland — Wailoa River State Park and the bayfront greens. The buildings were not replaced, the district was rezoned, and the town moved uphill.

One American city performed managed retreat sixty years before your book opens, and did it without a national argument, because the dead were recent and local and the decision was obvious to everyone involved.

**There are people alive in Hilo who remember the shops that used to be on that grass.** In a third act that spends decades arguing about whether retreat is thinkable, Hilo is the town that already knows the answer, and nobody outside Hawai'i ever asked it.

## The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center is at Ewa Beach, Oahu

The agency whose seismic trigger failed on Day 0 — no earthquake, therefore no origin, therefore no magnitude, therefore no warning — sits in the path of the wave it could not warn about.

It is on the south shore of Oahu, which faces away from the Ross beam and is partially shadowed. Whether you let it survive is a choice. Both options are usable, and the survivable version is probably crueller.

# 3. Hawai'i as a source

**This is the important section.** You asked whether something tectonic or volcanic could be imagined. It requires no imagination. Hawai'i is one of the most dangerous tsunami sources on Earth and it is already in motion.

## The Hilina Slump is moving now

Kīlauea's entire south flank, extending out to Cape Kumukahi, is sliding seaward. Parts of the central portion move as much as **10 cm per year**, driven by two forces at once: gravity, and the forceful injection of magma along the rift zone wedging the flank outward from inside.

It has failed abruptly before, twice in recorded history:

- **1868** — M7.9, the flank moved several to tens of metres, destructive tsunami
- **1975** — M7.2. A ~60 km wide section dropped several metres and slid seaward. Local runup ~15 m. Wave reached California with minor damage.

Surface expressions are visible and dramatic: the **Hilina Pali**, a set of arcuate cliffs that read as pre-collapse features, and the **Great Crack** on Kīlauea's southwest rift — 10 km long, up to 20 m wide and 20 m deep, plainly visible from the air.

## The scale available is far larger

Past Hawaiian failures dwarf 1975:

- **Mauna Loa's western flank** — individual debris-avalanche lobes travelled 100 km, with volumes exceeding 600 km³. All western flank landslides together exceed 2,000 km³.
- **Nuuanu slide** (Oahu) — carried blocks up to 10 km across some 50 km out to sea.
- Sidescan surveys of the Hawaiian EEZ have identified **70 landslides over 20 km in length** around the islands.

For comparison, the Prydz Channel Fan in your premise is ~2,900 km³. **Hawaiian flank collapse is the same order of magnitude as your Antarctic sources.**

## **The sentence that gives you licence**

From the USGS, on the Hilina and Punaluu slumps:

> The mechanisms by which these slowly creeping slumps fail catastrophically are unknown, as are the precursors to such activity.

**Nobody can tell you it is coming, and nobody can tell you it is not.** This is the published state of knowledge, and it means:

- You may fail the flank whenever the plot requires it
- A character who says *we do not know what the warning signs are, and we do not know whether there are warning signs* is quoting the literature, not hand-waving
- No monitoring scene is required, because no monitoring would help

That is a rare gift. Most catastrophes in fiction need either a warning system to defeat or an implausible surprise. This one is genuinely, professionally unpredictable.

## The triggering chain

Three separate perturbations on a structure already marginally stable:

1. **Pre-existing creep.** 10 cm/yr, magma-driven, ongoing at Year 0.
2. **The Ross wave crosses it** (~T+33 h). A 200-km wave swings bottom pressure across the submarine flank by metres of water head, for six hours. Same mechanism that fails the Bengal Fan on D+3.
3. **Four metres of permanent sea level rise.** This one is the strongest and the slowest. It loads the submarine flank continuously, and — more importantly — **raises the water table inside a volcanic edifice whose stability depends on pore pressure.** A wetter volcano is a weaker volcano. This is the TH-04 groundwater mechanism from Decades.md, operating on a mountain instead of a basement.

Plus **CX-05**: decompression melting increases magma supply globally, and magma injection is half of what drives the flank seaward in the first place.

**Timing latitude:** anywhere from T+33 h to Year 40. The physics supports all of it and the literature will not contradict you.

## Why this is the mechanism the book needs

The four Antarctic beams reach North America poorly. Prydz points up the Indian Ocean. Crary reaches the US eastern seaboard after 14,000 km. Ross beams up the 180th meridian toward Kiribati and the Aleutians. Amundsen reaches Chile strongly and California weakly and off-axis.

**Hawai'i points straight at North America — but only from the right flank.** This matters and is easy to get wrong.

A **Hilina** failure moves south-southeast, so it beams *away* from North America and toward Polynesia. Devastating for Tahiti, Samoa and the Cooks; not for California.

The failure that points at the Pacific Northwest is a **Nuuanu-type collapse off Oahu's northeast flank**, or a Molokai north-flank failure. Those do to California, Oregon, Washington and British Columbia what the Crary Fan does to the Atlantic seaboard — from 4,000 km, which is five and a half hours.

**If you want the West Coast specifically, use Oahu or Molokai, not Kīlauea.** See §3b for why each island fails in the direction it does.

So Hawai'i completes the geography. The Pacific coast of the United States spends the first act watching the Indian Ocean on television, and is then destroyed by a mountain in the middle of its own ocean that had been sliding into the sea, at ten centimetres a year, the whole time.

**Structural suggestion:** this is the second-act climax. Late enough that the reader has settled into the slow catastrophe of the sea level track, sudden enough to break that rhythm, and it relocates the story to the country that had been a spectator.

# 3a. Triggering — can the Antarctic event reach Hawai'i?

Four candidate mechanisms, ranked by what they buy.

### Weak: static stress transfer
The Ross fan failure is a slow event. It radiates seismic energy poorly and its static stress change at 10,000 km is negligible. Discard.

### Real but instantaneous: dynamic triggering
Passing seismic waves demonstrably trigger activity at great distance — the 2002 Denali earthquake set off seismicity thousands of kilometres away. But dynamic triggering acts during the seconds the waves pass. **If it does not fire immediately, it does not fire at all.** Useful only for a T+hours version, and it costs you the delayed one.

### Moderate: wave loading of the submarine flank
A 200-km wave crossing the flank swings bottom pressure by metres of water head, for six hours. Same mechanism that fails the Bengal Fan on D+3. Genuine, transient, one afternoon of opportunity.

### **Strong: the volcano's plumbing**

This is the mechanism worth building on, because it gives you both an immediate and a delayed version, and the delayed one is *observable*.

Kīlauea's flank slides because magma injected along the rift zone wedges it outward from inside. So flank stability is not purely mechanical — it is **magmatic**, and magma systems respond to stress changes.

Two documented effects:

- **Decompression melting.** Reduce pressure on the mantle and more of it melts. Iceland's eruption rate rose roughly thirtyfold after deglaciation.
- **Magma chamber sensitivity.** A chamber is a pressurised reservoir of compressible fluid. Small external stress changes can push it past the threshold for dike injection.

And note the fingerprint (SY-03 in Decades.md): Hawai'i is 10,000 km from Antarctica, deep in the **far field**, so it receives the global mean **plus 10–30%**. Four metres of water is roughly **40 kilopascals**, sustained, on the crust beneath the entire edifice and the mantle below it.

**The chain:** more magma supply → more frequent dike injection → faster flank wedging → **the 10 cm/yr creep accelerates.**

> **Why this is the best version: the flank speeds up in a way instruments can watch.**
>
> Someone puts the GPS creep rate on a chart next to the sea level curve and watches the correlation tighten for a decade. They still cannot predict the day — the USGS line about unknown precursors holds absolutely — but they can see it coming and be unable to say when.
>
> That is a far better decade of narrative than a surprise.

**The eruption-as-precursor variant.** Increased magma supply means increased eruption frequency, so the Big Island enters a period of unusual activity. Rift eruptions are *how the flank gets wedged*. A large one becomes the immediate trigger — which is what happened on smaller scales in 1975 and 2018.

### The honest limit, which a character should state

Increased eruption rate from unloading is documented for **ice caps sitting directly on volcanic provinces** — Iceland, where kilometres of ice came off the volcano itself. Extrapolating to 40 kPa of ocean loading on a hotspot volcano ten thousand kilometres from the ice is a real extrapolation. The mechanism is sound; the magnitude is unquantified.

**Make that a scene rather than hiding it.** The character arguing for the correlation is reasoning from established physics to an unmeasured case. The character doubting them is being properly rigorous. Neither is wrong, and the argument does not resolve before the flank does.

# 3b. The whole archipelago — water inside the mountains

## Water is already in there

Hawaiian basalt is extraordinarily permeable — fractured, rubbly, threaded with lava tubes. Every island carries a **freshwater lens** floating on seawater that has penetrated the edifice from below. Honolulu drinks from it.

**The deep interior of a Hawaiian volcano is saturated with seawater and always has been.** Nothing about sea level rise introduces water. It changes the pressure of water that is already present.

## Structure chooses the direction, and water follows it

A Hawaiian volcano has two **rift zones** forming a wedge. The flank not buttressed by a neighbouring volcano is the one that slides, and it slides roughly perpendicular to the rift.

- Kīlauea goes **south** because Mauna Loa blocks it to the north
- Koolau went **northeast** because that side faced open ocean

Rift zones are also the high-permeability pathways, so water enters along the same structures that control failure. **The leak follows the geometry rather than creating it** — which means it amplifies a direction already determined, and the direction for each island is a matter of published record rather than invention.

## Two clocks, and only one of them is yours

**Hydrothermal alteration** — hot water converting basalt to clay — is a documented cause of volcanic flank collapse worldwide. It takes **millennia**. Sea level rise does not create it.

**Pore pressure** is fast. Raise the water table, reduce the effective stress holding the rock together, and the flank weakens **this decade**. Same principle as the sediment fans in Sequence.md, operating on a mountain.

> **The honest and more useful version: the alteration is already there, left over from when the volcano was hot. Sea level rise pressurises it.**

That is better for the book, because it means **old, dead islands qualify too.**

## Therefore the archipelago unzips

Rising sea level raises the water table in every island **simultaneously**. Same forcing chain-wide. What differs is how close each was to failing:

| Island | State | Unbuttressed flank | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hawai'i (Big Island)** | Active, hot, magma-wedged | **South** (Hilina) | Most responsive by a wide margin. Beams into Polynesia. |
| **Oahu** | Extinct, cold | **Northeast** (Koolau) | Nuuanu slide proves this flank fails. Alteration already in place. **Beams at the Pacific Northwest.** |
| **Molokai** | Extinct | **North** | North shore sea cliffs are the largest on Earth — the headwall of an ancient collapse. Oversteepened. Beams toward Alaska and BC. |
| **Maui / Lanai / Kahoolawe** | Extinct | Various | Smaller, each with one unbuttressed side. |

**So you get a decade of collapses from one source region, each beaming somewhere different, with nobody able to say which island is next.** That is a far better structure than one large wave.

## The honesty check

Four metres of water is about **40 kilopascals**. The rock column inside Mauna Loa is tens of **megapascals** — your load is roughly a thousandth of the weight already sitting there.

**It cannot cause a collapse. It can only tip something already at the edge.** Which is precisely what a flank creeping at 10 cm/yr with no known precursors is.

Let a character say this out loud. It is the difference between a mechanism and a coincidence.

## The free consequence

The same rising water table **salinates the freshwater lens**. Honolulu loses its drinking water long before anything collapses.

That is TH-05 from Decades.md — a city rendered uninhabitable by water quality rather than flooding — arriving in the same place, from the same cause, as your second-act source. The island that destroys the West Coast is dying of thirst while it does it.

# 4. What people would notice

## As a target (Ross beam, ~T+33 h)

**They get warning.** More than a day, and the archipelago has the best tsunami preparedness culture in the United States — sirens, mapped evacuation zones, annual drills, inundation maps in every phone book. Hawai'i is the one place that is genuinely ready.

**And Hilo is ready in a way that will not be enough.** The sirens work, the evacuation works, the resonance still triples the wave.

**Local specifics:**
- Sirens on the first of the month are routine; sirens on any other day are not
- The bayfront parkland floods, as designed, because that is what it is for
- Kona-side moorings and the small-boat harbours are destroyed
- It repeats for hours; in 1960 the third wave was the killer
- Honolulu is partially shadowed on its south shore; the north shore of Oahu and the east coasts take the beam

## As a source

**Preceded by nothing.** That is the point. No swarm, no inflation signal, no precursor sequence — or if there is one, nobody knows what it looks like.

**An M7-plus earthquake as the flank goes**, which is itself a disaster on the Big Island before any water moves.

**Near-field runup of hundreds of metres** on the south coast of Hawai'i island. The 1975 event managed 15 m from a fractional slip.

**Then five and a half hours to California**, and a wave that has not dispersed.

# 5. Two disputes to keep honest

Both are real, published, and contested. **Neither should be asserted by the narration.** Both are ideal for two of your scientists to disagree about.

**Dispute 1 — the Lanai deposits.** Marine deposits are found high on Lanai and on other island flanks, and have been interpreted as runup from giant Hawaiian landslide tsunamis — with quoted wave heights in the hundreds of metres. Alternative explanations include tectonic uplift of the deposits after emplacement at lower elevation, and reef debris moved by other means. Wave deposits are also reported up to 100 m inland on the *northern* side of Lanai, which if it is runup implies waves wrapping around whole islands.

**Dispute 2 — the Australian evidence.** Geological evidence from the New South Wales coast has been attributed to a Hawaiian-generated tsunami around 100,000 years ago, with a wave starting near 375 m in Hawai'i and arriving at ~40 m in Australia. Those figures come from a specific interpretive tradition and are not consensus.

**How to use them:** a character who believes the Lanai deposits is arguing for a mechanism nobody has ever observed. A character who doubts them is arguing that the largest waves in the record are an artifact of misread geology. Both positions are held by real scientists. The disagreement is the scene, and it does not resolve.

## The lines to keep

Hawai'i is not a stop on the itinerary. It is the source that reaches America — already moving at ten centimetres a year, with no known precursors, in the middle of the ocean that spent the first act watching someone else drown.

And the sharpest framing of the whole mechanism:

> **The West Coast is destroyed by something that arrived as water, not as a wave.**
>
> Sea level rise pressurised the inside of a volcano ten thousand kilometres from the ice, fed its magma system, accelerated a flank that was already moving, and the flank did the rest. Nothing in that chain is something the first act would have recognised as a threat.
