# Timeline.1 — Measurable events from the collapse sequence

Master timeline. Every node is a thing an instrument records.

**Structure of each node:**
- **ID** — stable, for hanging narrative text off
- **Observable** — what the signal actually looks like
- **Instrument** — what records it, and who holds that data
- **Conf** — H (well established) / M (plausible, some literature) / S (speculative extrapolation)
- **Ref** — pointers to §Reference list

**Premise assumed:** four-sector collapse (Amery/Prydz, Ross, Weddell/Filchner-Ronne, Amundsen), shelves failing within ~12 h of each other by circumpolar wave triggering. Adjust node C-07 through C-10 if you change the ordering.

# A. PRECURSOR — T minus years to T minus zero

### A-01 | T–5y to T–1y | Grounding line retreat, shelf thinning
**Observable:** Grounding line migrating inland tens of metres to km/yr; shelf thinning cm to m/yr
**Instrument:** Differential InSAR (Sentinel-1); CryoSat-2 and ICESat-2 altimetry
**Conf:** H — this is currently being observed at Thwaites
**Ref:** R-20, R-21

### A-02 | T–months | Rift propagation across shelf
**Observable:** Rifts lengthening, visible in imagery; increasing rate
**Instrument:** Sentinel-1 SAR. **Critical: SAR works through cloud and polar night. Optical does not.** In austral winter, SAR is the only eye.
**Conf:** H
**Ref:** R-22

### A-03 | T–weeks | Icequake swarm
**Observable:** Hundreds of short-duration broadband cryogenic signals, 10–400 Hz, from ice-shelf fracturing
**Instrument:** Moored hydrophones near the front
**Conf:** H — **directly precedented.** Before the 2016 Nansen calving, moored hydrophones recorded this for weeks; the visible calving followed on satellite imagery much later.
**Ref:** R-01
**Note:** This is your opening chapter, already published. The ice announces itself acoustically before it announces itself optically.

### A-04 | T–days | Grounding-zone GPS acceleration
**Observable:** Displacement rate climbing from mm/day to cm/day
**Instrument:** GNSS array, grounding zone
**Conf:** H

### A-05 | T–hours to T–minutes | Rate excursion
**Observable:** mm → m within an hour; then loss of fix
**Instrument:** Same array, then nothing
**Conf:** M

# B. MINUTES — T+0 to T+60 min

### B-01 | T+0 | Fracture initiation
**Observable:** —
**Conf:** —

### B-02 | T+0 to T+3 min | Fracture front crosses 100 km
**Observable:** Propagation at 500–1,500 m/s. Ceiling is the Rayleigh wave speed in cold glacial ice, ~1,750 m/s; a crack tip cannot outrun its own stress field.
**Conf:** H
**Note:** "Speed of sound" in your draft is the correct order of magnitude for the *crack*. Not for the collapse behind it.

### B-03 | T+0 to +2 min | Seismic arrival — the first signal to leave
**Observable:** P waves at 6–8 km/s through the crust. Reaches global stations within minutes.
**Instrument:** Global Seismographic Network; IMS primary seismic
**Conf:** H
**Note:** **Three signals, three speeds, three bulletin streams.** Seismic (minutes) → hydroacoustic (~1 h) → infrasound (~4 h). Each correctly characterized, none assembled.

### B-04 | T+1–5 min | Long-period seismic signature
**Observable:** Energy 20–200 s. No impulsive onset. No coherent P. Amplitude rises over minutes, decays over tens of minutes. Does not resolve as fault rupture.
**Instrument:** GSN, IMS
**Conf:** H
**Ref:** R-02, R-23

### B-05 | T+2–10 min | Near-field wave
**Observable:** Steep, chaotic, short-wavelength; hundreds of metres runup in the embayment
**Instrument:** Nothing survives to record it
**Conf:** M
**Ref:** R-24 (Lituya Bay analogue)

### B-06 | T+10 min | Four stations lost
**Observable:** Simultaneous loss of comms — Davis (AU), Zhongshan (CN), Progress (RU), Bharati (IN)
**Instrument:** Absence of signal
**Conf:** H
**Note:** The first *human* datum is a silence, and it reads as a comms failure for hours.

### B-07 | T+15–40 min | Mélange formation
**Observable:** Rigid shelf → granular flow. Unjamming transition. Slabs capsizing into each other.
**Instrument:** Later SAR imagery only
**Conf:** H
**Note:** Not "gel" — that transition runs the other way. *Mélange*, *slurry*, *unjammed*.

### B-08 | T+52 min | Hydroacoustic arrival, HA01 Cape Leeuwin
**Observable:** Broadband, 11→4 Hz over ~340 s, back-azimuth 189°, amplitude exceeding station record
**Instrument:** IMS HA01 hydrophone triplet, SOFAR channel, 1,485 m/s over 4,660 km
**Conf:** H — cryogenic detections at HA01 are documented and catalogued
**Ref:** R-03, R-04, R-05

# C. HOURS — T+1 h to T+24 h

### C-01 | T+55 min | Automatic detection, IDC Vienna
**Observable:** STA/LTA trigger; feature extraction — azimuth, frequency, duration
**Instrument:** CTBTO International Data Centre
**Conf:** H
**Ref:** R-06

### C-02 | T+1–2 h | SEL1 issued
**Observable:** First automatic Standard Event List
**Conf:** H

### C-03 | T+2–3 h | Regional infrasound
**Observable:** Atmospheric pressure signal at Antarctic and Southern Ocean stations
**Instrument:** IMS infrasound (60 stations); on-ice arrays
**Conf:** M
**Ref:** R-07 — the Atka Bay study used seismic + hydroacoustic + on-ice infrasound together

### C-04 | T+3–5 h | Distant infrasound
**Observable:** ~340 m/s, but stratospheric ducting makes paths multi-branched. **Alternating zones of audibility and silence** — heard at 200 km, not at 80.
**Instrument:** IMS infrasound
**Conf:** H
**Note:** Not audible as pitch (below 20 Hz). Perceived as pressure, rattling glass, wrongness without direction.

### C-05 | T+4 h onward | Lamb wave / barometric pulse
**Observable:** Single positive pressure pulse, order 1–2 hPa, propagating at ~300–320 m/s
**Instrument:** WMO barometers; **home weather stations; smartphone barometers**
**Conf:** H — Hunga Tonga's pulse was recorded by over 1,300 barometers in Japan at 8,000 km, and by consumer sensors worldwide. Krakatoa was caught by ~50 barometers; Hunga Tonga by thousands.
**Ref:** R-08, R-09, R-10
**Note:** Best scene in the sequence. A few hundred thousand phones record it. The dataset exists for days before anyone knows what it is a dataset *of*. Hunga Tonga's pulse was detected at **Bharati** — the same station you destroy in B-06.

### C-06 | T+5–9 h | Southern Ocean DART buoys, first pulse
**Observable:** Amplitude under 4 cm, period under 6 min. Dispersive. Packet already fraying.
**Instrument:** DART bottom pressure recorders
**Conf:** H
**Note:** **This is the observation that justifies the cancellation.** It is correct, and it is correct about the wrong thing.

### C-07 | T+6.6 h | Ross front flexes → shelf 2 fails
**Observable:** Second hydroacoustic event, independent origin
**Instrument:** IMS; PTWC DART array
**Conf:** M — mechanism is precedented (see note), timing is arithmetic
**Ref:** R-11, R-12
**Note:** Circumpolar wave at √(gh) ≈ 713 km/h over 4,000 m. **Documented precedent: the Tohoku tsunami triggered calving at the Sulzberger Ice Shelf, 13,000 km away.** Also documented: Antarctic calving margins respond to swell generated by storms in the opposite hemisphere.

### C-08 | T+7.5 h | Weddell / Filchner-Ronne fails
**Conf:** M | **Ref:** R-11, R-12

### C-09 | T+9.6 h | Amundsen / Thwaites fails
**Conf:** M | **Ref:** R-11, R-12
**Note:** Physically the most likely sector to go first in reality. Reverse-sloping bed, MISI.
**Ref:** R-20, R-25

### C-10 | T+8–12 h | Predicted vs observed intervals
**Observable:** Predicted 6.6 / 7.5 / 4.6 h. Observed 6.75 / 6.4 / 5.0 h.
**Conf:** S — your invention, built on real propagation speeds
**Note:** The moment a character solves it on one sheet of paper.

### C-11 | T+11 h | Prydz Channel Fan fails
**Observable:** Long-period seismic, 15–30 min duration, no impulsive onset, sustained horizontal force
**Instrument:** GSN
**Conf:** M
**Note:** The delay is **pore-pressure diffusion** — water moving through mud after a sudden load change. Hours to days. This is the free gift of the whole structure: they survive the collapse, count the icebergs, think it's over.
**Ref:** R-13, R-26

### C-12 to C-14 | T+14–20 h | Crary, Ross, Belgica fans fail
**Conf:** M | **Ref:** R-13, R-26
**Note:** All four margins carry mapped trough-mouth fans. All four have failed before.

### C-15 | T+16–22 h | DART detects the *real* wave
**Observable:** Long period (~20 min), non-dispersive, amplitude order 0.5 m in deep water
**Instrument:** DART
**Conf:** H
**Note:** Interval between C-06 and C-15 is the whole first act.
