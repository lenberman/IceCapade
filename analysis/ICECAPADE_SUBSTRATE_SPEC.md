# ICECAPADE — Substrate Specification (v1)

> Adapted from `Critique/analysis/CRITIQUE_SUBSTRATE_SPEC.md` (v2). Critique's substrate faces backward: it keeps a finished manuscript answerable. IceCapade starts **empty** and grows chapter by chapter, so this substrate faces both ways: the same faceted, quote-anchored, git-current critique layer as Critique, plus a **forward-facing planning layer** (`PLAN.md`) for what has not been written yet. The author writes all prose; the substrate's roles are continuity keeper and critic-on-demand.

---

## 0. Purpose and philosophy

The product is a substrate that can (a) keep the growing manuscript internally consistent — chronology, character facts, promises made to the reader — and (b) answer complex literary questions about it at any stage (negative review of the partial draft, stress test, pitch, character study), cheaply re-verified as the text changes.

Design commitments, inherited from Critique:

1. **Faceted, not totalizing.** Knowledge is split into durable facets of atomic claims; different questions consume different facets.
2. **Question-driven coverage.** "Done" is measured against `QUESTIONS.md`, not against "fully theorized."
3. **Currency by construction.** The manuscript is versioned in git; every claim is anchored to verbatim text and tagged with the chapters it depends on, so an edit yields a targeted re-verification list, not a global re-read.

New commitment for a book being written:

4. **The plan is a facet, not a decree.** `PLAN.md` records intent — arc, planned chapters, promises to pay off. When a new chapter contradicts the plan, that is a *finding*, not automatically an error: the resolution is either revise the chapter or revise the plan, and the author decides. The substrate's job is to make the divergence visible immediately, not months later.

### The three layers (unchanged)

- **Manuscript** = read-only ground truth (`html/`, under git). The final arbiter for everything written.
- **Facets** = durable knowledge, atomic claims, anchored to the manuscript — except `PLAN.md`, which is anchored to intent and therefore carries no quote anchors until its material lands on the page.
- **Dashboard** = volatile orchestration only. No findings; status, coverage, queues.

## 1. Repository layout

```
IceCapade/
  lyx/                      # authoring sources (LyX); not ground truth
  html/                     # manuscript, ground truth: one HTML file per chapter, exported from lyx/
  reference/                # research & source materials (images, articles, notes); input, never ground truth
  analysis/
    ICECAPADE_SUBSTRATE_SPEC.md  # this file
    DASHBOARD.md                 # orchestration; no findings
    QUESTIONS.md                 # registry of answerable question types + readiness
    facets/
      PLAN.md                    # forward-facing: premise, arc, planned chapters, open promises
      BOOK_FACTS.md              # roster, relationships, chapter map, key settings (written text only)
      THEMES.md                  # what recurs, where, to what end
      WEAKNESSES.md              # standing ledger of soft spots, with severity
      POSITIONING.md             # genre, audience, comps, pitch
      READER_EXPERIENCE.md       # naive first-read model of the draft so far
      ARCHITECTURE.md            # structural reading, as structure emerges
    evidence/                    # deep scoped reports (CRA_*, Arc_*, Motif_*) as they accumulate
    renders/                     # dated deliverables rendered from facets
    IceCapade_timeline.xlsx      # timeline workbook (created at first scaffold; dormant until then)
    timeline/                    # CSV mirror, regenerated by tools/timeline_export.py
  tools/                    # ported from Critique: anchors.py, timeline_*.py, build_reader.py
  prompt.txt                # operating prompts (blocks A/B/C/D/P/K/T/U)
  commit.sh  REMOTE_SETUP.md  .gitignore
```

`lyx/` is where writing happens; `html/` (the LyX HTML export, same filename per chapter) is what the substrate reads, diffs, and anchors to — identical to the Critique workflow. `reference/` holds source material the novel draws on; facets may cite it informally (`reference/<file>`), but nothing in `reference/` is ever an anchor.

## 2. The facets

Same atomic-claim format as Critique (§4 there): ID'd, 1–3 sentences, self-anchoring, self-describing (`depends`, `status`), YAML front-matter with `depends_on_chapters`, `feeds_questions`, `verified_against`. Facets are refined in place, never journaled.

| Facet | Holds | Primary consumers |
|---|---|---|
| `PLAN.md` | Premise, intended arc, planned chapter list (working titles + one-line intents), **open promises** (setups the text has made or the plan requires, each with where it must pay off), structural intentions. IDs `PL-##` for plan claims, `PR-##` for promises. | continuity audit, drafting the next chapter, pitch |
| `BOOK_FACTS.md` | Neutral ground truth of the *written* text: roster + relationships, chapter map, chronology in prose, settings. No interpretation, no intentions. | nearly every question |
| `THEMES.md` | Themes and motifs as they actually appear on the page. | reviews, essays, comps |
| `WEAKNESSES.md` | Standing ledger of soft spots with severity; grows the audience dimension (Critique §6.1) when the draft is far enough along to have audiences. | negative review, stress test, defense |
| `POSITIONING.md` | Genre, register, audience, comps, pitch. Early entries may derive from `PLAN`; mark those `basis: plan` until the text supports them. | comps, jacket copy, pitch |
| `READER_EXPERIENCE.md` | Naive first-read model of the draft so far: confusion points, withholds, pacing, setup/payoff tracking. | stress test, pacing notes |
| `ARCHITECTURE.md` | The structural reading, as structure becomes real. | structural essays, defense |

**The PLAN/BOOK_FACTS boundary is the substrate's central discipline.** BOOK_FACTS states only what the text says; PLAN states only intent. When a chapter lands, material moves: a planned beat that is now on the page leaves `PLAN` (or its promise is marked **paid**, with the anchor) and enters `BOOK_FACTS` with a quote anchor. A promise the text pays off differently than planned is a divergence finding (§7.1).

### 2.1 The timeline

Identical mechanics to Critique §2.1 — canonical `.xlsx` workbook, diff-able CSV mirror via `tools/timeline_export.py`, rows carrying a chapter dependency, validated by `anchors.py`, styled/synced by `timeline_sync.py`. **Dormant until the first chapters exist.** When the book's chronology takes shape, scaffold the workbook (sheet names and calendar conventions defined then — the ported tools currently carry Critique's sheet-name prefixes and will be adjusted in the same pass) and record the calendar conventions in `BOOK_FACTS` and in this section.

## 3. Anchoring convention (quote-canonical, unchanged)

Verbatim quoted phrase + chapter is the canonical anchor: `"distinctive phrase of real prose" (ChapterName)`. Line numbers are drift-prone cache only. ≥ ~6 words, unique in the chapter. `tools/anchors.py` flags any quote it can no longer find — the staleness signal. `PLAN.md` claims carry no quote anchors by design; everything else does.

---

## 4. The dashboard — orchestration only

As Critique §5: coverage map, staleness queue, work queue, pointers. One addition: a **divergence queue** (§7.1) listing open plan-vs-text conflicts awaiting an author decision. Readable in minutes or it has failed.

---

## 5. The question registry

As Critique §6, seeded in `QUESTIONS.md`. Most entries start **GAPS** (their facets are empty) and flip to READY as the draft grows. One question type is new:

```
### Q-CONT — Continuity audit
needs:     the plan; the facts of the written text; the timeline
consumes:  PLAN (primary), BOOK_FACTS, timeline
renders:   renders/Continuity_<date>.md  (or inline findings on ingest)
readiness: tracks PLAN + BOOK_FACTS currency
```

Q-CONT runs implicitly on every chapter ingest (§7) and on demand for a full sweep. Audience-parameterized rendering (Critique §6.1) is deferred until `WEAKNESSES` is mature enough to carry a matrix.

---

## 6. Currency workflow (git-driven, unchanged core)

When `html/` changes: detect via `git diff --name-only <last ms tag> -- html/`; localize dependent claims via `depends_on_chapters` + `anchors.py`; re-verify (intact / reword / retire); re-baseline `verified_against`; commit manuscript and analysis separately; tag `ms-YYYY-MM-DD`. The timeline rides the same cycle once live.

### 7.1 Ingest of a NEW chapter (the primary flow here)

Extends Critique's "prompt B" with plan reconciliation:

1. Detect + `git add` the new file(s); read them.
2. **Extend BOOK_FACTS first** (chapter map, roster, dates), then the interpretive facets as warranted — atomic, quote-anchored claims.
3. **Reconcile against PLAN**: mark paid promises (with anchor); list every divergence — a beat that contradicts the plan, a promise paid differently, a new promise the chapter opens (add as `PR-##`). Divergences go to the dashboard's divergence queue for the author's call; the substrate never silently rewrites the plan to match the text or vice versa.
4. Timeline pass (once live), lint, separate commits, `ms-` tag.

---

## 8. Bootstrapping

This project starts **ab initio** (Critique §8): there is nothing to migrate. The bootstrap order is: author seeds `PLAN.md` (a conversation or a draft outline dropped in `reference/` works as input); `POSITIONING` takes what `PLAN` implies, marked `basis: plan`; everything else waits for text. The first real chapter triggers §7.1 and begins filling `BOOK_FACTS` and `READER_EXPERIENCE`.

---

## 9. Operating prompts

In `prompt.txt`: **A** ingest modified chapter · **B** ingest new chapter (with plan reconciliation) · **C** ask a question / render · **D** autonomous next pass · **P** plan work (seed or revise `PLAN.md` with the author) · **K** continuity sweep (full Q-CONT) · **T/U** timeline (dormant until scaffolded).

---

## 10. Integrity (lint)

`python3 tools/anchors.py` — identical checks to Critique §10: every anchored quote found in current HTML; cross-facet claim IDs resolve; every `html/` chapter covered by BOOK_FACTS (a new chapter shows MISSING until ingested — the safety net); QUESTIONS `consumes` facets exist; timeline rows resolve and mirror fresh (once live). Only facet STALE anchors gate the exit code.

---

## 11. Health

At a given `ms-*` tag the substrate is healthy when the staleness queue is empty, the divergence queue is empty *or explicitly parked by the author*, lint passes, and every `QUESTIONS.md` entry is READY **or** GAPS-for-lack-of-text (a gap the draft has not reached yet is not ill health; a gap the draft has outgrown is).
